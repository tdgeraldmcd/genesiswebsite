04 / YOUR FREE CLIPS

NORTH: Novelty — Make People Stop and Pay Attention

There's a reason most people scroll past the majority of content they encounter.

They've seen it all before.

The same advice.

The same talking points.

The same recycled ideas repackaged into another carousel, reel, or LinkedIn post.

And when something feels familiar, it's easy to ignore.

That's where novelty comes in.

Use this direction to introduce something new or different into your clips.

Sometimes it's a genuinely new idea.

Other times, it's a familiar idea presented in a way nobody has seen before.

Either way, the goal is the same:

Give your audience a reason to stop and pay attention.

But attention alone isn't enough.

Which brings us to the second direction.

05 / YOUR FREE CLIPS

WEST: Wisdom — Make People See Their Problems Differently

Here's a little secret about great thought leadership.

The most valuable thing you can give someone isn't necessarily more information.

It's a better way of understanding their problem.

Think about it.

If someone is struggling to get clients from social media, you could tell them:

"Post three times a week."

That might be useful advice.

But what if you asked them something different?

"What if your problem isn't how often you post, but that your content gives people no compelling reason to hire you?"

That's an entirely different conversation.

Now they're no longer thinking about posting schedules.

They're thinking about the quality and substance of their message.

You've changed their perspective.

And in doing so, you've positioned yourself as someone who understands their problem on a deeper level.

You can create this effect in your clips by:

  • Old Way vs. New Way: Show your audience why the conventional approach may be holding them back.
  • Why You're Struggling: Explain the hidden reason someone hasn't solved their problem.
  • The Doctor's Office: Start with symptoms and problems before introducing a solution.
  • Personal Experience: Turn lessons you've learned into insights others can apply.
  • Metaphors and Analogies: Make complicated ideas simple and memorable.
  • Naming Your Ideas: Give your concepts recognizable names that people can remember and repeat.

These approaches help you go beyond merely sharing facts.

They help you become someone whose perspective your audience actively seeks out.

SOUTH: Story — Make Your Expertise Memorable

Have you ever listened to someone explain a concept and forgotten it five minutes later?

Now think about a great story you heard years ago.

Chances are, you still remember it.

Stories have a way of making ideas stick.

They give people something to imagine.

Something to relate to.

Something to feel.

And when you're selling a service, that's especially important.

Because your potential clients aren't simply evaluating information.

They're evaluating whether they understand you, trust you, and believe you can help them.

Use a simple storytelling structure to turn your own experiences into compelling clips.

You don't need to be a professional writer.

You don't need to have an extraordinary life story.

You just need to understand how to take an experience, identify the conflict, and reveal the lesson.

And once you can do that, ordinary experiences become opportunities to demonstrate your expertise.

Let's say someone discovers your content.

The important thing is that your content doesn't simply end after sharing an insight.

It gives your audience a logical next step.
